We have numerically studied the transient behavior of the evolution of Field Reversed Configuration (FRC) plasma with a rotating magnetic field(RMF). We consider the evolution of the plasma formed by a field reversed theta pinch by increasing the internal magnetic flux with an RMF and by regulating the axial magnetic field. The dynamic responses of a plasma pressure, separatrix radius and length are obtained by solving the radial force balance and the energy balance on the assumption that a current profile is preserved. We have studied the conditions for a successful evolution on the initial plasma parameters, the time variations of the RMF angular frequency and the axial magnetic field. It has been shown that the internal magnetic flux can be increased by applying an RMF with time-varying frequency and controlling the axial magnetic field appropriately

## 4 Summary and Conclusions

It is shown that even if the RMF frequency is asynchronous initially with the electrons, the electrons gradually follow the RMF resulting in the complete penetration of RMF into the plasma. As a result, the RMF can increase the plasma current and enhance the internal magnetic flux. It is also shown that the input power by the RMF is supplied through the Joule loss of the azimuthal plasma current because the azimuthal plasma current is much larger than the axial current except for the initial transient period.

The evolution of the configuration driven by the RMF was studied assuming the rigid rotor model of the current profile and using the zero dimensional model of the particle, momentum and energy balance. In order to sustain the configuration and make it evolve successfully, the power balance between the RMF input power and the loss power must be almost satisfied during the transition. The time evolutions of the length and radius of the configuration strongly depend on the above power balance. The elaborate controls of the RMF angular frequency and the external magnetic field are required. It is found that the configuration can be sustained for time much longer than the energy confinement time by suitable choice of the controls.
